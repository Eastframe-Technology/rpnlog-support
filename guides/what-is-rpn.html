What is RPN, and how do you enter it?

RPN, or Reverse Polish Notation, puts the numbers first and the operation last. Instead of 3 + 4 =, you key 3 ENTER 4 +. There is no equals key and there are no brackets, because the calculator keeps your numbers on a stack.

The name comes from the Polish notation of the logician Jan Łukasiewicz, which wrote the operator before its numbers. RPN reverses that, so the operator comes after.

The stack

An RPN financial calculator holds four numbers, called X, Y, Z and T. You can think of them as a pile, with X at the bottom. X is the one on the display and the one you are typing into. A two-number operation like + uses X and Y, puts the result in X, and drops everything else down one level.

ENTER is what separates two numbers. It copies X up into Y, and the next number you key replaces X.

Notice what happened after the first +. The 7 stays in X, and when you key 5 it moves up on its own. You did not have to write it down or use brackets. The last × finds the 7 and the 11 already waiting. That is nine key presses, against twelve on a calculator with brackets and an equals key.

For a chain of steps you often need only one ENTER. For 250 ÷ 4 × 1.2 − 15:

250 ENTER 4 ÷ 1.2 × 15 − → 60

The keys that make it work

KeyWhat it does
ENTERSeparates two numbers. Pressing it again copies X further up the stack.
CHSChanges the sign of X. This is how you key a negative number, since − is subtraction: 100 CHS is −100.
x⇄ySwaps X and Y. Useful when you entered two numbers the wrong way round for a division.
R↓Rolls the stack down one level, so you can see what is in Y, Z and T.
LSTxBrings back the last X used by an operation, which undoes a wrong operation or reuses a number.
CLxClears X to 0, so you can re-key it.

The mistakes everyone makes

  • Pressing ENTER before the operation. 3 ENTER 4 ENTER + gives 8, not 7, because the second ENTER copied 4 into Y and the + used both 4s. ENTER goes between numbers, and the operation itself finishes the entry.
  • Using − for a negative number. It subtracts instead. Use CHS.
  • Expecting an answer to wait for equals. The operation runs the moment you press it.
  • Getting the order backwards. For division and subtraction, the first number you key is the one being divided or subtracted from. 12 ENTER 3 ÷ is 4.

Percentages

The % key works out a percentage of Y and leaves Y in place, so the next operation can use it. To add 15% to 200:

200 ENTER 15 % → 30   + → 230

Why people stay with it

There is nothing to parse in your head. You work a formula from the inside out, one piece at a time, and every intermediate result is visible as you go. If you got something wrong, you see it at that step and not at the end. It takes some practice at first, mostly to stop looking for the equals key.
